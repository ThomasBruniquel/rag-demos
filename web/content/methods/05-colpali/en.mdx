need: Find information in scanned pages or pages full of diagrams, where extracted text isn't enough.
how:
  - label: Snapshot
    text: Each page is handled as an image, with no text extraction step.
  - label: Match
    text: A vision-language model splits the page into small patches and matches each one against the words of the question.
  - label: Show
    text: The answer displays the page and figure it found, so the user sees the source.
strengths:
  - Diagrams, tables and layout count in the search.
  - No fragile OCR chain to maintain just to find the right page.
  - The source is shown as it is, which builds trust.
limits:
  - A heavier index than a text index (several vectors per page).
  - The model needs more compute than a text embedding model.
choose:
  - Technical manuals, drawings, scanned reports, older documents.
  - Answers that live in a figure or a table.
avoid:
  - "Clean, born-digital documents: OCR or direct extraction is enough."
  - Millions of pages on a very tight budget.
adaptation: "The project compares four routes on the same questions: Textract OCR followed by text RAG, with or without figure descriptions, and visual retrieval with Cohere Embed v4 (one vector per page, live on Bedrock) or with ColQwen (ColPali family, many vectors per page). ColQwen needs a GPU to encode every question: it is computed once, for the evaluation and the example questions."
