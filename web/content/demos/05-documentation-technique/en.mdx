---
title: Technical Documentation
tagline: Find a procedure or a diagram in scanned reports, and see the figure it comes from.
problem: "For a manufacturer with decades of manuals and technical reports, often scanned, full of diagrams and tables. The answer to a maintenance question sometimes lives only in a figure, which text extraction ignores."
corpus:
  name: NASA Technical Reports Server (NTRS)
  size: "70 reports, 3,450 pages, including 37 scans from 1963 to 1985"
  licence: "U.S. Government works, public use permitted (NTRS)"
  url: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/
examples:
  - "Show me the hydraulic circuit of the antenna drive: at what pressure are the relief valves set?"
  - Which component rejects heat in the Stirling convertor tested in thermal vacuum?
  - What were the operating conditions of the mainshaft seals during the 150-hour endurance run?
  - Show me a photo of the lubrication pump shaft and gear assemblies.
architecture:
  alt: "Architecture: two families of routes compared on the same questions. OCR route: text read by Textract and figure descriptions, indexed in S3 Vectors. Visual route: pages indexed as images, by Cohere Embed v4 live and by ColQwen (ColPali-style) computed offline. The answer shows the cited page and figure."
  choices:
    - title: Compare OCR and visual retrieval
      why: Depending on the document, either one wins; the project shows it on the same questions instead of claiming it.
    - title: Show the cited figure
      why: For a maintenance question, seeing the diagram beats reading a description.
    - title: OCR quality control
      why: Pages with low Textract confidence are flagged for review.
    - title: One embedding model for text and images
      why: "Between the OCR route and the visual route only the input changes: the comparison is about the method, not about two models."
  discarded:
    - title: OCR only
      why: Loses the information in diagrams, which is often what people are looking for.
    - title: A ColPali-style model queried live
      why: It needs a GPU to encode every question, beyond the budget; ColQwen is computed once, for the evaluation and the example questions.
pipeline:
  alt: "Pipeline: upload to S3, EventBridge, one Step Functions run per report: scanned or born-digital detection, text from Textract (scans) or from the PDF (born-digital), figures found by Textract and described by Claude, embeddings of the text and of the page images, S3 Vectors index."
  facts:
    - label: Volume
      value: "70 reports, 3,450 pages"
    - label: Ingestion time
      value: "~2 h"
    - label: Ingestion cost
      value: "≈ €19"
    - label: Pages flagged for review
      value: OCR confidence below 80%
evaluation:
  measured: false
  caption: Hand-checked questions whose answer is in a figure, a table or the text, over old scans and recent reports.
  columns: [Route, "Figures (right page, top 5)", "Scans (right page, top 5)", Cost per question]
  rows:
    - ["OCR, text only", "25%", "60%", "€0.0003"]
    - [OCR + figure descriptions, "50%", "65%", "€0.0003"]
    - [Page images (Cohere Embed v4), "65%", "60%", "€0.0005"]
    - ["Page images (ColQwen, offline)", "75%", "65%", "€0.0005"]
mission:
  duration: "Proof of concept in 2 weeks, production in 4 to 6 weeks"
  scope:
    - An assessment of your documents (scans, diagrams, quality)
    - OCR versus visual retrieval compared on your questions
    - Figures displayed in answers
    - Quality control and a review queue
limits:
  - It doesn't read CAD drawings, only PDF documents and images.
  - Badly degraded scans remain hard for every approach.
  - The ColQwen route is only available for the example questions.
  - The reports are in English; questions can be asked in French.
seo:
  title: Multimodal RAG over scanned technical documents
  description: "Project: search 3,450 pages of NASA reports, scanned and recent, with diagrams. Textract OCR versus visual retrieval (Cohere Embed v4, ColQwen), with figures shown in the answer."
---

Rather than choosing blindly between OCR and visual retrieval, the project puts them head to head on the same questions, and shows the figure that backs up the answer.
