---
title: Financial Analyst
tagline: Accurate figures from annual reports packed with tables.
problem: "For analysts, auditors and finance teams who spend hours hunting for a figure in 300-page reports, then comparing it across years or companies. A standard assistant chops tables into pieces and reads the wrong row; here, a wrong figure is not an option."
corpus:
  name: 10-K annual reports from FinanceBench (open subset)
  size: "~50 reports, ~10,000 pages, with reference questions and answers"
  licence: "CC BY-NC 4.0 (non-commercial use, to be confirmed before launch)"
  url: https://github.com/patronus-ai/financebench
examples:
  - What was the operating margin of the company in FY2022, and how did it change from FY2021?
  - Compare capital expenditure between the two most recent fiscal years.
  - Which segment contributed the most revenue?
  - What is the debt-to-equity ratio at year end?
architecture:
  alt: "Architecture: the Lambda function shows the report's table of contents to the model, which picks a chapter then a section; the chosen sections (whole tables) are read, calculations go to a tool, and the answer cites the section."
  choices:
    - title: Navigation through the table of contents (inspired by STAIR)
      why: Annual reports are highly structured; drilling down chapter by chapter keeps tables whole and cites a precise section.
    - title: Three PDF extractors compared
      why: "Extraction is the real problem: plain text, Docling (open source) and Textract are compared on the same questions."
    - title: A calculation tool
      why: The model doesn't do mental arithmetic; changes and ratios are computed by a function, then cited.
  discarded:
    - title: Fixed-size chunks
      why: They cut tables into pieces without headers, the main cause of wrong figures.
    - title: Training a model on the reports, as in the STAIR paper
      why: Expensive, and has to be redone for every new report; the training-free variant keeps most of the gain.
pipeline:
  alt: "Pipeline: PDFs on S3, Step Functions processes each report: extraction (Textract on table pages, Docling elsewhere), table of contents rebuilt, section-based chunking, context enrichment, S3 Vectors index and metadata in the Glue Data Catalog."
  facts:
    - label: Volume
      value: "~50 reports, ~10,000 pages"
    - label: Ingestion time
      value: "~3 h"
    - label: Ingestion cost
      value: "€20 to €40"
    - label: Pages sent to Textract
      value: Table pages only
evaluation:
  measured: false
  caption: FinanceBench reference questions; an answer is correct when the figure is exact.
  columns: [Set-up, Exact figures, Table cited in full, Cost per question]
  rows:
    - [Classic RAG (plain text), "54%", "31%", "€0.004"]
    - [Classic RAG (Textract), "63%", "52%", "€0.004"]
    - [Table of contents + Textract + calculator, "79%", "88%", "€0.011"]
mission:
  duration: "Proof of concept in 2 weeks, production in 4 to 6 weeks"
  scope:
    - An extractor benchmark on your own documents
    - Structure-based navigation and a calculation tool
    - An evaluation set built with your analysts
    - An ingestion pipeline for new reports
limits:
  - It gives no investment advice.
  - The reports are US filings (10-K format), in English.
  - Documents without a usable table of contents fall back to standard search.
seo:
  title: RAG over financial PDF reports with tables
  description: "Project: find and compare figures in 300-page annual reports, with retrieval guided by the table of contents (STAIR method, IBM Research) and verified calculations."
---

The project compares approaches honestly: three PDF extractors, with and without structure-based navigation. Tables stay whole, the answer cites the section, and calculations are done by code, not by the model.
