problem: "An insurer, a quality department or a safety team holds thousands of incident reports. The useful questions are rarely \"what does this report say?\" but rather \"which factors come up most often?\" or \"which incidents combine this equipment with that maintenance issue?\". Classic RAG, which only reads a few passages, can't answer them."
corpus:
  name: NTSB aviation accident database (National Transportation Safety Board)
  size: "~3,000 reports: structured data and investigation narratives"
  licence: Public domain (United States)

examples:
  - What factors come up most often in approach-phase accidents?
  - Which accidents involve both a single-engine aircraft and a maintenance issue?
  - How are weather conditions linked to loss of control in flight?
  - Summarise the main causes of runway excursions.
architecture:
  alt: "Architecture: for the same question, two answers side by side. On the left, classic RAG over the narratives. On the right, a search in the Neo4j graph: community summaries for global questions, graph traversal for multi-hop questions."
  choices:
    - title: Side-by-side display
      why: Showing the difference on the same question is more convincing than claiming it.
    - title: A fixed entity schema for extraction
      why: "Aircraft, flight phase, cause, factor, location: a fixed schema prevents a messy graph and makes checking easier."
    - title: Validation against structured data
      why: The official NTSB fields, queried with Athena, are used to check the entities the model extracts.
    - title: Three variants compared
      why: GraphRAG, LightRAG and RAPTOR have different costs and strengths; the demo publishes the comparison.
  discarded:
    - title: Neptune (AWS graph database)
      why: Fixed cost too high for the site's budget; Neo4j AuraDB Free is enough for 3,000 reports.
    - title: Free-form extraction without a schema
      why: Produces thousands of unusable entity types.
