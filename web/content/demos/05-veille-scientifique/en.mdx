examples:
  - Recent work on retrieval-augmented generation evaluation, since 2025?
  - Which papers compare graph-based and vector-based retrieval?
  - This week's papers on language model quantisation?
  - Who works on Matryoshka embeddings, and what did they find?
architecture:
  alt: "Architecture: the question becomes a compressed vector, a fast first search in S3 Vectors with date and field filters, then precise reranking of the top 100 candidates before answering."
  choices:
    - title: Truncated, quantised Matryoshka vectors
      why: The index shrinks to a fraction of its original size; storage and search stay very cheap at a million documents.
    - title: Two-stage search
      why: A fast pass over compressed vectors, then precise reranking, recovers most of the lost precision.
    - title: Batch embeddings
      why: Bedrock batch inference costs about half as much as single calls for the initial load.
  discarded:
    - title: Full-precision, full-length vectors
      why: An index more than ten times larger for a small precision gain once reranking is in place.
    - title: An always-on search engine
      why: A fixed monthly cost incompatible with the site's budget.
pipeline:
  alt: "Pipeline: initial load with Step Functions Distributed Map and Bedrock batch inference; every day, EventBridge triggers fetching new arXiv papers, de-duplication by hash, embedding and index update."
  facts:

The challenge here isn't the model but running it at scale: a replayable initial load, an idempotent daily update, and a compressed index that keeps storage under a euro a month.
