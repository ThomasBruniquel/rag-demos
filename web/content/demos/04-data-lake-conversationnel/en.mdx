title: Conversational Data Lake
tagline: Ask 20 million rows a question in plain language, and see the SQL that answers it.
problem: "For a company whose data already sits in a data lake, but where every business question goes through the data team and waits its turn. Managers want to query the figures themselves, without writing SQL, and without the risk of a wrong number circulating in meetings."
corpus:
  name: DVF property transactions (Demandes de valeurs foncières), data.gouv.fr
  size: "20.4 million rows, i.e. 7.3 million French property sales from 2021 to 2025"
  licence: Etalab Open Licence 2.0
examples:
  - Median price per m² for flats in Lyon in 2024, by district?
  - How many houses were sold in Gironde in 2023, and at what average price?
  - Which towns in the Ain department saw the steepest rise in price per m² since 2021?
  - "Nombre de ventes à Paris par an ?"
architecture:
  alt: "Architecture: the question triggers a search over table descriptions and validated examples, generation of several candidate SQL queries, their validation and read-only execution by Athena, then selection of the best one and a summary with a table and a chart."
  choices:
    - title: Several candidate queries, then selection
      why: A single query sometimes gets a join or a filter wrong; comparing several results makes the error visible.
    - title: RAG over the data catalogue
      why: The model only sees the tables, columns and examples relevant to the question, not the whole schema.
    - title: SQL guardrails
      why: "The API role can only read four tables (Lake Formation), every query is checked before it runs, the Athena workgroup stops any query that would scan more than 1 GB, at most two corrections."
    - title: Parquet partitioned by year and department
      why: A question about one department and one year scans a few megabytes; Athena then costs less than a tenth of a cent per question.
    - title: One row per sale, prepared by the pipeline
      why: "In the DVF files, a sale spans several rows and the same property can be repeated: counting rows overstates sales almost threefold. The pipeline settles this once and for all, instead of leaving the model to reinvent it for every question."
  discarded:
    - title: Giving the model the whole schema
      why: More expensive, and the model mixes up similarly named columns.
    - title: An always-on database
      why: Pointless for occasional use; Athena bills per query.
    - title: A Glue Crawler
      why: "The schema is known, and a crawler would overwrite the hand-written descriptions. The tables are declared in code, and Athena works out the partitions itself."
pipeline:
  alt: "Pipeline: raw DVF CSV files copied to S3, cleaned, typed and grouped by sale by Glue ETL, written as partitioned Parquet, tables declared in the Glue Data Catalog with partition projection, data checked by Athena; table descriptions, the glossary and validated examples are indexed in S3 Vectors."
  facts:
    - label: Volume
      value: "20.4 million rows, 7.3 million sales"
    - label: Ingestion time
      value: "~15 min (Glue)"
    - label: Ingestion cost
      value: "≈ €0.15 (Glue Flex)"
    - label: Athena cost per question
      value: "< €0.001"
    - label: Parquet storage
      value: "930 MB, < €0.05 per month"

mission:
  duration: "Proof of concept in 2 weeks, production in 4 to 6 weeks"
  scope:
    - An audit and write-up of your data catalogue

This is the most data-engineering-heavy project on the site: answer quality depends first on the pipeline (partitioned Parquet, a documented catalogue, read-only permissions), and only then on the model that writes the SQL.
